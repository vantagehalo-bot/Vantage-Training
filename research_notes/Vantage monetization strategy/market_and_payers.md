# Market Size and Payers for Blind / Low-Vision Assistive Navigation Technology (Vantage)

Research date: 2026-11-16. Research budget was capped at about 15 tool calls, so several payer routes are listed under Gaps instead of being verified. Every number below has a source URL. Anything not sourced is labelled as unverified background.

## 1. How many blind / low-vision people are there (global and US, UK, EU, India)?

### Takeaway
About 43 million people worldwide are blind and 295 million have moderate-to-severe vision impairment (Lancet Global Health / VLEG 2020 estimates, used by WHO and IAPB). That is about 338 million people who could use a navigation aid. In the countries that matter here: US about 7 million with vision impairment (about 1 million blind), UK more than 2 million living with sight loss (about 320k registered), India about 4.95 million blind and 70 million vision impaired.

### Cited Findings
- Globally at least 2.2 billion people have a near or distance vision impairment, and for at least 1 billion of them it could have been prevented or has not been addressed. — [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/blindness-and-visual-impairment)
- About 43 million people are blind and 295 million have moderate-to-severe vision impairment. Rates are higher in women than in men. — [IAPB Vision Atlas](https://visionatlas.iapb.org/topics/causes-of-sight-loss/)
- By degree: 295M moderate-to-severe (3.5% of world population), 258M mild (3.3%), 510M near vision impairment (6.5%), 43M blind (0.5%). — [Haileamlak, Ethiop J Health Sci, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9554782/)
- 77% of blindness and of MSVI is preventable or treatable (about 33M of the blind, about 260M of MSVI) (2020/2021 data). — [Orbis summary of Lancet GH study](https://www.orbis.org/en/news/2021/new-global-blindness-data)
- Older WHO-adopted estimate for comparison: 253 million people were visually impaired in 2015. — [Lighthouse Guild](https://lighthouseguild.org/newly-published-data-adopted-as-official-who-recognized-estimates-for-the-prevalence-of-blindness-and-vision-impairment/)
- **US:** about 7 million people in the US have vision impairment. — [CDC Fast Facts](https://www.cdc.gov/vision-health/data-research/vision-loss-facts/index.html). More than 7 million live with uncorrectable vision loss, including more than 1 million who are blind. — [Prevent Blindness](https://preventblindness.org/prevalence-visual-acuity-loss-blindness-us/). State-level modelled estimates are available from CDC VEHSS, e.g. Mississippi with 66,000 vision loss + 7,000 blind = 73,000. — [CDC VEHSS](https://www.cdc.gov/vision-health-data/prevalence-estimates/vision-loss-prevalence.html)
- US self-report (a much broader definition): 18.4% of US adults are blind or have "some" or "a lot" of difficulty seeing even with corrective lenses (CDC NHIS 2022). — [EEOC](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/visual-disabilities-workplace-and-americans-disabilities-act)
- **UK:** more than 2 million people live with sight loss severe enough to affect daily life, and about 320,000 of them are registered as Sight Impaired or Severely Sight Impaired (CVI). — [RNIB key statistics](https://www.rnib.org.uk/professionals/research-and-data/key-information-and-statistics-on-sight-loss-in-the-uk/)
- **India:** an estimated 4.95 million blind people and 70 million vision-impaired people, of whom 0.24 million are blind children (2022 paper). — [Mannava et al. 2022, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9359234/)

### Inferences
- The realistic primary users of an obstacle-detection wearable are the blind plus severe low-vision groups, not the 2.2B headline figure. That is roughly tens of millions globally and roughly 1–2M in the US. Pitch decks that use the 2.2B figure will lose credibility.
- India has the largest absolute pool of potential users but the lowest ability to pay (see Q3/Q4). The US and UK have smaller pools but third-party payers.

### Gaps
- An EU-wide or per-country EU prevalence figure was not retrieved. The IAPB Vision Atlas country pages have it but were not fetched.
- The India National Blindness & Visual Impairment Survey 2015–19 primary report (blindness prevalence of about 1.99% among people aged 50+) was not fetched. The 4.95M figure comes from a secondary paper.
- WHO/Vision Atlas updates after 2020 (e.g. 2025 Vision Atlas refresh) were not checked.

## 2. Assistive tech for the visually impaired: market size and growth (and the navigation segment)

### Takeaway
Analyst estimates put the global "assistive technologies for the visually impaired" market at about US$5.7–7.8B for 2025–26, with growth claims of 7–13% CAGR. The estimates differ widely and come from paid-report vendors whose methods are not public, so treat them as rough figures only. No reliable public figure was found for the navigation/mobility-aid segment on its own.

### Cited Findings
- $6.87B (2025) → $7.76B (2026), 12.9% CAGR. — [The Business Research Company](https://www.thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/assistive-technologies-for-visually-impaired-global-market-report) (the same figures are resold via [ResearchAndMarkets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/5948769/assistive-technologies-visually-impaired-market))
- $6.34B (2025) → $7.09B (2026), 11.86% CAGR. — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/assistive-technologies-for-visually-impaired-market)
- $5.66B (2025) → $10.8B by 2034, 7.45% CAGR. — [The Insight Partners](https://www.theinsightpartners.com/reports/assistive-technologies-for-visually-impaired-market)
- Forecast of $20.89B in 2034, 13.17% CAGR from 2029. — [Yahoo Finance press release (ResearchAndMarkets/TBRC)](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/assistive-technologies-visually-impaired-market-081400355.html)
- Vision impairment segment at $4.95B in 2022, 8.83% CAGR (Coherent Market Insights). — [BioSpace press release](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/assistive-technology-market-size-to-worth-usd-32-25-billion-by-2030-coherent-market-insights)
- Total assistive technology market (all disabilities) projected to reach about $88B by 2035, 10.22% CAGR. — [Market Research Future](https://www.marketresearchfuture.com/reports/assistive-technology-market-29777)

### Inferences
- The estimates for the same year (2025) range from $5.66B to $6.87B, and the CAGRs range from 7.45% to 12.9%. That spread is typical of desk-research reports. A defensible figure to cite is "about $6–7B (2025), roughly 10% growth (analyst estimates, low reliability)".
- These totals are dominated by screen readers, magnifiers/CCTVs, braille displays and smart glasses. Navigation/mobility aids (white canes, electronic travel aids, smart canes, wearables) are probably a small slice, perhaps under $1B. This is an inference, not sourced.
- A bottom-up estimate is more credible for Vantage. Example: about 1M blind people in the US × payer-funded share × device ASP.

### Gaps
- No free, sourced figure for the "electronic travel aid / smart cane / navigation wearable" segment. Segment splits exist only behind paywalled reports.

## 3. Willingness and ability to pay: employment, income, out-of-pocket spend

### Takeaway
In the US only about 44% of people with visual impairment are employed, against 77% of people without disabilities. In the UK the employment rate of people with sight loss has been falling since 2018. Ability to pay out of pocket is limited, which is why third-party payers (VA, vocational rehabilitation, Access to Work, ADIP) dominate this market.

### Cited Findings
- About 44% of the US population with a visual impairment were employed and 10% were unemployed. The comparison groups are 77% employed / 5% unemployed for people without disabilities and 35% / 12% for people with other disabilities. More than half are not in the labour force. The study calls the common "70% unemployment" figure incorrect. — [McDonnall, NRTC on Blindness & Low Vision, Mississippi State](https://www.blind.msstate.edu/sites/www.blind.msstate.edu/files/2024-01/Article_Summary_-_Employment_and_Unemployment_Rates_of_Peopl.pdf)
- 46.2% of people with visual impairments were employed in 2019, compared with 78.6% of people without disabilities. — [Kanwar et al. 2024, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11298568/)
- An older estimate gives a 37% employment rate for legally blind / visually impaired adults (2015). — [Bell, NFB JBIR 2015](https://nfb.org/sites/default/files/images/nfb/publications/jbir/jbir15/jbir050202.html)
- AFB publishes annual employment statistics (2023–2024 figures on its page). — [AFB employment statistics](https://afb.org/research-and-initiatives/statistics/employment-bvi)
- UK: the employment rate for people with sight loss has been falling since 2018, even though it improved for other groups (2022 data, published Aug 2024). — [RNIB](https://www.rnib.org.uk/professionals/research-and-data/reports-and-insight/employment-of-people-with-sight-loss-in-2022/)
- Price anchor for the low end: the IIT Delhi SmartCane retails for about $34 / Rs 3,000. — [Borgen Project](https://borgenproject.org/smartcane/), [Eyeway](https://www.eyeway.org.in/?q=smart-cane)

### Inferences
- A direct-to-consumer model in the US/UK is constrained by income. Many users are older and retired, since vision loss is age-related. The fastest revenue is more likely payer- or institution-funded (VA, VR agencies, schools, employers) or B2B/B2G (NGOs, CSR in India).

### Gaps
- No sourced figure on median income or typical annual out-of-pocket assistive-tech spend for blind people in the US, UK or India.
- The exact AFB 2024 employment percentages were not extracted, because the snippet was garbled.

## 4. US payers

### Takeaway
The VA is the strongest US payer. It gives approved devices to eligible blind and low-vision veterans at no cost to the veteran. For example, all OrCam devices for veterans are VA-provided. Medicare is largely closed to these devices because of the statutory "eyeglass exclusion". Schools fund materials through the APH Federal Quota (about $420–$490 per registered student). State vocational rehabilitation agencies and ADA employer accommodations are further routes, but this pass did not verify them.

### Cited Findings
- **VA Blind Rehabilitation Service:** VA provides blind and visual impairment rehabilitation services to eligible veterans and active-duty service members, and calls itself the first and only national healthcare system to provide this fully. — [VA Prosthetics BRS](https://www.prosthetics.va.gov/PROSTHETICS/blindrehab/index.asp)
- "Approved equipment and technology is provided by VA Prosthetics Service at no cost to the patient." — [VA About BRS](https://www.rehab.va.gov/blindrehab/About_Blind_Rehabilitation_Service.asp)
- Precedent: "All OrCam devices are provided by the VA, and veterans with VA health care coverage who are low vision or legally blind may qualify to receive a fully covered" device. OrCam is also a Blinded Veterans Association sponsor. — [BVA – OrCam](https://bva.org/sponsor/orcam/)
- VA also runs an Assistive Technology programme for evaluation, intervention and training. — [VA AT fact sheet](https://www.prosthetics.va.gov/factsheet/AT-FactSheet.pdf), [VA Assistive Technology](https://www.prosthetics.va.gov/AssistiveTechnology/index.asp)
- VA does not provide guide dogs directly but works with non-VA agencies that do. — [VA.gov blind/low vision rehab](https://www.va.gov/health-care/about-va-health-benefits/vision-care/blind-low-vision-rehab-services/)
- **VA procurement routes:** by law (38 U.S.C. § 8123) the VA Secretary may procure prosthetic appliances "by purchase, manufacture, contract, or in such other manner as the Secretary may determine to be proper, without regard to any other provision of law". — [US Code](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section8123&num=0&edition=prelim)
- VA Federal Supply Schedule 65 II A (Medical Equipment & Supplies) has an open solicitation (RFP-797-FSS-99-0025-R10). — [VA OPAL FSS 65 II A](https://www.va.gov/opal/nac/fss/schedule65iia.asp). Contracted items are searchable in the VA NAC Contract Catalog. — [VA NAC catalog](https://www.vendorportal.ecms.va.gov/nac/medsurg/list)
- **Medicare:** CMS ruled that devices that "aid vision or provide magnification of images for impaired vision" are excluded under the statutory "eyeglass" exclusion. — [AFB](https://afb.org/blindness-and-low-vision/disability-rights/medicare-medicaid-and-social-security); [CMS/regulations.gov document](https://downloads.regulations.gov/CMS-2010-0101-0197/attachment_2.pdf). The exclusion covers technology using "one or more lens for the primary purpose of aiding vision", and ACB lobbies to repeal it. — [ACB](https://www.acb.org/content/low-vision-device-exclusion-legislative-imperative). Appeals for lens-based low-vision devices are unlikely to succeed (2026 guide). — [VisionBuddy](https://visionbuddy.com/does-medicare-cover-low-vision-devices/). JAMA Ophthalmology argued the exclusion should not be read broadly. — [JAMA Ophthalmology](https://jamanetwork.com/data/journals/OPHTH/6969/esa05009_1350_1357.pdf)
- **Schools / APH Federal Quota:** APH runs an annual census of eligible blind and low-vision students to set per-student funding. — [APH Federal Quota](https://www.aph.org/federalquota/). The per-capita amount was $420 per registered student (Wisconsin, date not given). — [WCBVI](https://wcbvi.k12.wi.us/news/outreach/clarifications-on-the-use-of-the-aph-federal-quota-fund/). It was $488.53 per student for 2021–22 registrants, giving New Hampshire about $158k for 324 students. — [NH DoE FY24 presentation](https://www.education.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt326/files/inline-documents/sonh/fy24-federal-quota-census-training-presentation.pdf). The 2025 distribution list is published. — [APH 2025 distribution](https://www.aph.org/app/uploads/2026/09/2025-Distribution-of-Eligible-Students.pdf)

### Inferences
- An obstacle-detection wearable that uses a camera and no lens for magnification arguably falls outside the Medicare "eyeglass" exclusion, but coverage is still unlikely without a DME benefit category and HCPCS code. Do not plan around Medicare.
- The VA is the closest US analogue to a "single payer that buys novel blind tech" (the OrCam precedent). Realistic entry route: get VA BRS clinicians and Blind Rehab Centers to trial the device, then sell via open-market prosthetics purchases under § 8123 authority or FSS 65 II A. The FSS route takes longer and requires a SAM registration and commercial sales history. This is an inference from the sources above.
- APH Federal Quota funds can only buy from the APH catalogue. A vendor would need APH to carry the product, which is a partnership route rather than a direct sale (inferred; the APH page says funds are for "educational materials").

### Gaps
- **State vocational rehabilitation (VR):** purchasing rules, vendor approval and typical spend were not retrieved. RSA-911 data and state agencies for the blind (e.g. Texas, Massachusetts) were not fetched.
- **Medicaid:** state-by-state coverage of electronic mobility aids was not verified.
- **Private insurance:** no sourced data. It is generally excluded, but this was not verified.
- **ADA employer accommodation:** JAN (askjan.org) cost data (e.g. median accommodation cost) was not fetched.
- **IDEA:** device funding through IEPs and school districts was not verified beyond the APH Federal Quota.

## 5. UK payers

### Takeaway
Access to Work is the UK's best-funded route for people in work. It pays up to £69,260 per person per year (the cap is unchanged for 2024–25, 2025–26 and, per secondary sources, 2026–27). It covers specialist equipment over and above the employer's reasonable-adjustment duty. The scheme has serious processing backlogs.

### Cited Findings
- Access to Work grant cap: £69,260 a year for grants awarded or reviewed 8 Apr 2024–31 Mar 2025 and 1 Apr 2025–31 Mar 2026. — [GOV.UK Access to Work factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-work-factsheet/access-to-work-factsheet-for-customers)
- The same £69,260 cap applies for 1 Apr 2026–31 Mar 2027 (secondary sources). — [Inkfire](https://inkfire.co.uk/how-much-is-access-to-work-grant/), [Recallify](https://recallify.ai/access-to-work/)
- Access to Work is a demand-led grant in Great Britain. It can pay for specialist equipment, support workers, travel to work and workplace adaptations, over and above the employer's duty to make reasonable adjustments. Awards are typically approved for 3 years. A Public Accounts Committee report highlights processing delays and backlogs. — [UK Parliament PAC report](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5902/cmselect/cmpubacc/92/report.html)

### Inferences
- Access to Work buys equipment per individual on the recommendation of an assessor. Getting Vantage into assessor recommendations, for example through UK AT resellers, is the practical route. Retirees, who are the majority of people with sight loss, are not eligible.

### Gaps
- NHS provision: low-vision services generally supply optical aids, not electronic navigation aids. Not verified.
- Local authority sensory services (rehabilitation officers, long canes): funding amounts not retrieved.
- Disabled Students' Allowance 2025/26 or 2026/27 caps: not retrieved.
- RNIB grants and Thomas Pocklington or Blind Veterans UK schemes: not retrieved.

## 6. EU payers (national reimbursement examples)

### Takeaway
Germany's statutory health insurance (GKV) explicitly includes electronic orientation and mobility aids and obstacle detectors ("Hindernismelder") in product group 07 "Blindenhilfsmittel" of the Hilfsmittelverzeichnis, most recently updated in October 2025. That makes Germany the clearest EU reimbursement path for an obstacle-detection device. France funds technical aids through the MDPH/PCH at up to €3,960 per 3 years.

### Cited Findings
- **Germany:** product group 07 "Blindenhilfsmittel" (updated 15 Oct 2025) includes electronic aids for orientation and mobility, including obstacle detectors ("Hindernismelder") that protect against collisions. — [GKV-Spitzenverband PG 07 update](https://www.gkv-spitzenverband.de/media/dokumente/krankenversicherung_1/hilfsmittel/fortschreibungen_aktuell/2025_3/2025_10_15_Fortschreibung_Produktgruppe_07_Blindenhilfsmittel.pdf)
- PG 07 orientation and mobility aids include long canes, electronic orientation and mobility aids, and guide dogs. — [REHADAT-GKV Hilfsmittelverzeichnis](https://www.rehadat-gkv.de/info/index.html?pgnr=7&pginfo=true)
- GKV pays for aids needed in the individual case to compensate for a disability. The Hilfsmittelverzeichnis is compiled by GKV-Spitzenverband and is not exhaustive, so unlisted aids can also be reimbursed. Prior approval by the insurer is generally required. The patient co-pays 10% (minimum €5, maximum €10). Prices are negotiated between insurers and suppliers, up to fixed amounts (Festbeträge). Insured persons use suppliers who hold contracts with their insurer. — [German Federal Ministry of Health (BMG)](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/hilfsmittel)
- DBSV, the German blind association, explains GKV benefits under PG 07. — [DBSV](https://www.dbsv.org/viii-leistungen-der-krankenkassen.html)
- **France:** the PCH "aide technique" element usually gives up to €3,960 per 3 years, with higher amounts possible for higher-tariff aids. — [MDPH 13](https://www.mdph13.fr/demarches/Pages/AideTechnique.aspx). Health insurance (Assurance maladie) reimburses aids listed on the LPPR when prescribed, and the PCH can cover the difference. — [Paris PCH guide (2017)](https://handicap.paris.fr/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Guide-PCH-AT_2017_A4-V8_HD-ss-deb.pdf), [Portail Handicap](https://portail-handicap.fr/droits-demarches/pch-aides-techniques)

### Inferences
- Germany is the most structured EU market. Listing in the Hilfsmittelverzeichnis requires an application to GKV-Spitzenverband with proof of function, safety (CE/MDR) and medical benefit. Entry also requires contracting with insurers via a "Leistungserbringer", i.e. a specialist dealer. CE/MDR class I medical-device status would likely be needed. The application requirements are inferred and were not verified.

### Gaps
- Netherlands (WMO / Zvw hulpmiddelen) and Nordics (municipal/regional hjälpmedelscentraler in Sweden, NAV Hjelpemiddelsentral in Norway) were not researched.
- German listing procedure details (§139 SGB V application, timelines) were not fetched.

## 7. India payers (ADIP, ALIMCO, CSR, NGOs, SmartCane precedent)

### Takeaway
The central government's ADIP scheme gives eligible people with disabilities (monthly income ≤ ₹22,500) free aids worth up to ₹15,000 once every 3 years. For some aids the ceiling is ₹30,000–₹50,000, and people earning ₹22,500–30,000 get a 50% subsidy. ALIMCO is the main implementing agency. The IIT Delhi SmartCane (about ₹3,000 / about $34) is on ADIP and has been distributed in camps. That is a direct precedent for a low-cost Indian electronic mobility aid reaching scale through government plus NGO channels.

### Cited Findings
- ADIP eligibility: monthly income from all sources ≤ ₹22,500 gives free aids, and ₹22,501–30,000 gives 50% cost. The ceiling is ₹15,000 once every 3 years, rising to ₹30,000 or ₹50,000 for some appliances. ALIMCO is the main pan-India implementing agency. — [ALIMCO ADIP brochure](https://alimco.in/WriteReadData/UserFiles/file/ADIP_Brochure7KZE.pdf)
- A valid UDID or enrolment with a minimum benchmark disability of 40% is required. — [ALIMCO FAQ guide](https://alimco.in/WriteReadData/CMS/Awareness.pdf)
- Under ADIP, grants-in-aid go to implementing agencies: ALIMCO, National Institutes, and others. — [DEPwD ADIP page](https://depwd.gov.in/en/adip/)
- Camp precedent: ALIMCO distributed aids worth over ₹1.75 crore under ADIP in a single camp, including Smart Canes and smartphones. — [PIB press release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1591471)
- NGO channel: NAB (National Association for the Blind) Chandigarh & Punjab held an ADIP assessment camp with ALIMCO. — [NAB Facebook post](https://www.facebook.com/100066743179069/posts/national-association-for-the-blind-nab-in-collaboration-with-alimco-recently-hos/986501123584651/?locale=zh_TW)
- CSR channel: BEL (a PSU) handed over ALIMCO-made assistive devices under CSR. — [BEL Facebook video](https://www.facebook.com/BharatElectronicsLimited/videos/csr-bel-has-handed-over-assistive-devices-manufactured-by-alimco-artificial-limb/618286367739465/)
- **SmartCane:** distributed via 40+ channel partners and in 12+ other countries, and part of the ADIP scheme of the Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment. — [IIT Delhi Assistech SmartCane](https://assistech.iitd.ac.in/smartcane.php). Price is about ₹3,000. Saksham Trust is the dealer and Assistech IIT Delhi the manufacturer. — [Eyeway](https://www.eyeway.org.in/?q=smart-cane). Retails for about $34. — [Borgen Project](https://borgenproject.org/smartcane/). The design target for user modules was under $50. — [IIT Delhi paper](https://assistech.iitd.ac.in/doc/smart-cane-paper6.pdf)

### Inferences
- For India the ADIP ceiling (₹15,000 generally) sets the price frame. A Vantage wearable would need a variant at or under about ₹15,000 (about $175) to be fully funded per beneficiary. A ₹30,000–50,000 ceiling might apply if listed in a higher category, but this is unverified.
- The SmartCane path was: IIT incubation → NGO partner (Saksham) → ALIMCO/ADIP listing → camp distribution plus CSR. That is the proven playbook. Partnering with an existing ADIP-listed entity or NGO is likely faster than direct empanelment.
- PSU and corporate CSR-funded camps (e.g. BEL with ALIMCO) are a realistic early B2B revenue source in India: a bulk buyer, often co-run with ALIMCO.

### Gaps
- **How a new device gets listed under ADIP / ALIMCO procurement:** not found. Neither ALIMCO document explains vendor or product empanelment. The ALIMCO tender portal and DEPwD ADIP guidelines (2022 revision) should be checked.
- **Companies Act Schedule VII wording:** not fetched. Item (ii) covers "promoting education, including special education ... among ... differently abled"; this is unverified background, so confirm it on mca.gov.in.
- Accessible India Campaign (Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan) relevance to device funding: not researched.
- State schemes (e.g. state disability welfare departments) and Saksham's own sales volumes: not found.
- SmartCane cumulative units sold: not found on the snippets. The IIT page may have it.

## 8. How a new device or app gets onto these funding lists (cross-cutting)

### Takeaway
Each payer has a different gate. The VA buys under § 8123 open-market authority or FSS 65 II A contracts. Germany requires listing (or individual approval) in the GKV Hilfsmittelverzeichnis plus a contracted supplier. India routes through ALIMCO/ADIP implementing agencies. UK Access to Work pays per individual based on assessor recommendations. The fastest route for a small vendor is usually individual purchases (VA prosthetics, VR counsellors, Access to Work), which need no formal list.

### Cited Findings
- VA statutory flexibility to buy prosthetic appliances "without regard to any other provision of law". — [38 U.S.C. § 8123](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title38-section8123&num=0&edition=prelim)
- VA FSS 65 II A solicitation and NAC catalogue. — [VA OPAL](https://www.va.gov/opal/nac/fss/schedule65iia.asp), [VA NAC catalog](https://www.vendorportal.ecms.va.gov/nac/medsurg/list)
- Germany: the Hilfsmittelverzeichnis is not exhaustive and unlisted aids may also be reimbursed. Insurers require prior approval and contracted suppliers. — [BMG](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/hilfsmittel)
- India: ADIP funds flow via implementing agencies (ALIMCO, National Institutes, NGOs). — [DEPwD](https://depwd.gov.in/en/adip/)

### Inferences
- Fastest-to-revenue ranking by entry barrier:
  1. Individual-authorisation payers that need no listing: VA prosthetics purchase for a single veteran, UK Access to Work, US state VR.
  2. NGO/CSR bulk purchases in India.
  3. Formal listings: FSS, GKV HMV, ADIP empanelment, APH catalogue. These take months to years.
- Inference only. Rank 1 still requires a clinician or counsellor to recommend the product, so evidence and trials with BRS or VR staff matter.

### Gaps
- No primary source was retrieved for state VR vendor approval, ADIP empanelment procedure, the GKV listing application process or APH product partnerships. These are the highest-priority follow-ups.
